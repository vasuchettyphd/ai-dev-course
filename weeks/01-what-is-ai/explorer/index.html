What AI is: an explorer

Everything on this page comes from a real AI model (Qwen2.5-0.5B, a small open model). Nothing here is made up. Try the three activities below. You do not need to install anything, and you do not need to be online.

1. Guessing the next word

A language model does one thing: it scores every possible next piece of text. Pick a sentence start, type the word you think comes next, then press Reveal to see what the model thought.

1.0

Temperature is a dial for how adventurous the model is. Low means it sticks to its favourite word. High means it spreads its bets. At 1.0 these are the model's real numbers. At other settings the page estimates them, treating all the words outside the top 50 as one group.

2. How the model sees text

Before a model reads anything, it chops the text into pieces called tokens. Each coloured chip below is one token. A dot (·) marks a space at the start of a piece.

Models read tokens, not letters or words. That's why counting the r's in strawberry can trip them up.

3. Where the model looks

While reading a sentence, the model decides how much each earlier word matters to the word it is on. That is called attention. Three small experiments follow.

Models park a lot of attention on the first word as a kind of resting place; we hide it by default so the rest is easier to see.
